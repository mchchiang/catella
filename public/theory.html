Theory

Model Overview

The system consists of a one-dimensional lattice of length \(L\), representing DNA base-pair positions. Nucleosomes occupy a footprint of size \(w\).

The configuration of the system at time \(t\) is described by the occupancy field:

(1)\[n_i(t) \in \{0,1\}, \quad i = 1, \dots, L\]

where \(n_i = 1\) indicates that site \(i\) is covered by a nucleosome.

Energy Function

The total energy of a configuration is defined as:

(2)\[E = \sum_{i=1}^{L} V_i n_i + \sum_{i<j} U(|i-j|) n_i n_j\]

where:

  • \(V_i\) is the sequence-dependent potential

  • \(U(r)\) is an interaction potential

Metropolis Sampling Dynamics

We evolve the system using a Metropolis Monte Carlo scheme.

For a proposed move with energy change \(\Delta E\), the acceptance probability is:

(3)\[P_{\mathrm{acc}} = \min \left( 1, e^{-\beta \Delta E} \right)\]

where:

\[\beta = \frac{1}{k_B T}\]

The algorithm ensures detailed balance with respect to the Boltzmann distribution:

(4)\[P(\{n_i\}) \propto e^{-\beta E}\]

Mean Occupancy

The mean occupancy profile is defined as:

(5)\[\langle n_i \rangle = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} n_i(t)\]

This quantity corresponds to the time-averaged coverage at base-pair \(i\).

Relationship to Output

In the implementation:

  • \(\langle n_i \rangle\) is stored in analysis["occupancy"]

  • the energy trajectory \(E(t)\) is stored in analysis["energy"]

Equation (3) is implemented in the C++ core of the simulation engine.
