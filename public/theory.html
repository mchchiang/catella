Simulation Model

We model the DNA fiber as a one-dimensional (1D) lattice array given by coordinates \(x \in \{0,\ldots,L-1\}\), where \(L\) is the total length of the DNA fiber (in units of bp). Within the simulations, nucleosomes of size \(\ell_{\text{nuc}}\) are considered.

The Energy of the System

The effective Hamiltonian of the system has three contributions:

\[H = E - \mu N \equiv E_{\text{seq}} + E_{\text{rep}} -\mu N \;,\]

where \(E_{\text{seq}}\) is an energy associated with the methylation footprinting signal (see below), and \(E_{\text{rep}}\) is a pairwise repulsion energy due to the excluded volume of the nucleosomes (i.e., they cannot overlap). The last term is a chemical energy associated with a non-sequence-specific nucleosomal binding energy to the DNA fiber, with \(\mu\) being the chemical potential (note that a positive \(\mu\) means it is favourable for nucleosomes to bind to the fiber) and \(N\) the total number of nucleosomes on the fiber.

To obtain \(E_{\text{seq}}(x)\), we first compute the probability \(p(x)\) of a nucleosome binding to the DNA fiber at \(x\) based on the methylation footprinting data. If control data are available (i.e., footprinting data for a fully methylated and unmethylated fiber), we define a normalized methylation score \(M(x)\) as follows:

\[M(x) = \frac{\langle S_{\text{test}} \rangle_{\text{bin}}(x) - \langle S_{\text{unmeth}} \rangle_{\text{bin}}(x)} {\langle S_{\text{meth}} \rangle_{\text{bin}}(x) - \langle S_{\text{unmeth}} \rangle_{\text{bin}}(x)} \;,\]

where \(S_{\text{test}}\), \(S_{\text{meth}}\), and \(S_{\text{unmeth}}\) are the methylation signal strengths as computed by ModKit (i.e., the value from the mod_qual column; defined for each bp) for the test condition (chromatinized DNA), a fully methylated fiber, and an unmethylated DNA fiber, respectively. Here, the operation \(\langle\cdot\rangle_{\text{bin}}\) denotes that we have first smoothed the signal by doing a rolling average with a bin size of \(\ell_{\text{bin}}\). Typically, we take \(\ell_{\text{bin}} = 147\) bp, which is the typically length of DNA wrapped around a nucleosome [1]. If control datasets are unavailable, we set

\[M(x) = \langle S_{\text{test}} \rangle_{\text{bin}}(x) \;.\]

To convert \(M(x)\) into a formal probability that lies between 0 and 1, we rescale the score linearly, setting those ranking below 1% to 0 and those ranking above 99% to 1, i.e.,

\[p(x) = 1-\frac{\widetilde{M(x)} - M_{p=0.01}}{M_{p=0.99}-M_{p=0.01}} \;,\]

where

\[\widetilde{M(x)} = \text{min}[\text{max}[M(x),M_{p=0.01}],M_{p=0.99}] \;.\]

Finally, we convert \(p(x)\) to the energy assuming it follows a Boltzmann distribution:

\[E_{\text{seq}} = \text{min}[-k_BT\log p(x), E_{\text{seq}}^{\text{max}}] \;,\]

where \(k_B\) is the Boltzmann constant, \(T\) is the temperature of the system, and \(E_{\text{seq}}^{\text{max}}\) is a threshold maximum energy that the user can specify to avoid \(E_{\text{seq}} \to \infty\).

To model the pairwise repulsion between two nucleosomes (say \(i\) and \(j\)), we consider the following potential:

\[\begin{split}E_{\text{rep}}^{ij}(x_{ij}) = \begin{cases} \infty & \text{if } x_{ij} < \ell_{\text{nuc}} \\ k_BT\left[\left(\frac{\ell_{\text{link}}}{x_{ij}-\ell_{\text{nuc}}}\right)^{12} - 2\left(\frac{\ell_{\text{link}}}{x_{ij}-\ell_{\text{nuc}}}\right)^6 + 1\right] & \text{if } \ell_{\text{nuc}} \leq x_{ij} \leq \ell_{\text{nuc}}+\ell_{\text{link}} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \;, \end{cases}\end{split}\]

where \(x_{ij} = |x_i-x_j|\) is the separation between the nucleosomes, \(\ell_{\text{nuc}} = 147\) bp, and \(\ell_{\text{link}}\) is the typical length of the linker DNA between nucleosomes (set to 50 bp). Under the condition \(\ell_{\text{link}} < \ell_{\text{nuc}}\), only nearest neighbour interactions are effective, and so

\[E_{\text{rep}} = \sum_{i=0}^{N-2}E_{\text{rep}}^{i,i+1}\]

for \(N \geq 2\) and 0 otherwise.

Monte Carlo Sampling Moves

Since we allow the number of nucleosomes to vary, the statistical ensemble sampled is the grand canonical ensemble. In thermal equilibrium, the probability of observing a configuration \(\mathcal{C}\) is

\[P(\mathcal{C}) = \frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\exp[-\beta H(\mathcal{C})] \;,\]

where \(\beta = 1/(k_BT)\) and \(\mathcal{Z}\) is the partition function. We perform one of the following three moves in each step:

  1. Shifting a nucleosome We randomly select the \(i\)-th nucleosome and move its position \(x_i \to x_i' = x_i \pm 1\) bp. The acceptance probability is:

    \[p(x_i\to x_i') = \min\left[1,\exp[-\beta(E(x_i')-E(x_i))]\right] \;.\]

  2. Adding a nucleosome We randomly select a position \(x\). The probability of accepting a new nucleosome at that position is:

    \[p(\text{add at } x) = \min\left[1,\frac{L}{(N+1)\ell_{\text{nuc}}}\exp[\beta\mu-\beta\Delta E_{\text{add}}(x)]\right] \;,\]

  3. Removing a nucleosome We randomly select and remove the \(i\)-th nucleosome. The acceptance probability is:

    \[p(\text{remove } i) = \min\left[1,\frac{N\ell_{\text{nuc}}}{L}\exp[-\beta\mu-\beta\Delta E_{\text{remove}}(x_i)]\right] \;.\]

These moves follow the Metropolis algorithm, ensuring detailed balance and thermal equilibrium.

Footnotes

We perform the rolling average in a left-aligned manner, so \(\langle S \rangle_{\text{bin}}(0) = \frac{1}{\ell_{\text{bin}}} \sum_{x=0}^{\ell_{\text{bin}}-1}S(x)\). For \(x \geq L-\ell_{\text{bin}}\), we set the score to be same as \(\langle S \rangle_{\text{bin}}(L-\ell_{\text{bin}}-1)\). This has no effect as the nucleosome cannot “fall off” the fiber.
